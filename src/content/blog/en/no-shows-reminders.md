## A no-show isn't just an empty slot

When a client doesn't turn up, the salon loses several things at once:

- **the money for the service** that didn't happen;
- **the stylist's time**, sitting idle and earning nothing;
- **another client** who wanted exactly that time but was turned away.

And the most frustrating part: most no-shows aren't people changing their minds. They simply forgot, mixed up the day, or couldn't make it and felt awkward writing in.

## Let's do the maths

Take a small studio:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Bookings per month | 120 |
| Average ticket | $15 |
| No-shows | 1 in 12 bookings |

That's **10 no-shows a month**, or **$150** the salon didn't earn. Over a year, **$1,800**. Plug in your own numbers: even if you have half as many no-shows, the amount is still noticeable.

## Why the receptionist can't keep up with reminders

Reminding every client means messaging or calling 120 times a month, at the right time, without missing anyone. In practice reminders happen "when there's a minute", and on busy days they don't happen at all. Those are exactly the days with the most no-shows.

## How bot reminders work

The bot reminds **every client, every time, at the right moment**:

1. **A day before the visit:** "Reminder: tomorrow at 2:30 pm, manicure with Olena". With **"I'll be there"** and **"Reschedule or cancel"** buttons.
2. **An hour before the visit:** a short reminder so the client leaves on time.

The key is the reschedule button. A client whose plans changed doesn't need to call and explain: one tap moves the booking. And the freed slot is **immediately available** to other clients again. Instead of a no-show, the salon gets a new booking.

## What it gives you

- fewer no-shows, because clients don't forget;
- fewer empty slots, because cancellations come in advance, not "5 minutes before";
- the receptionist doesn't spend hours on reminder calls;
- the spreadsheet shows who confirmed their visit and who didn't.

## What reminders cost

Reminders are sent automatically on Telegram, so unlike SMS you don't pay per message. Running such a bot costs a few dollars a month. If it saves even one no-show, it has already paid for itself.
